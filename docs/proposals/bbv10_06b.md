## 11. Statistics — percentile-first, envelope-aware

### 11.1 Percentile is the primary statistic

For each scored metric, the candidate is placed against the selected peer
distribution (section 9) and reported as a percentile:

- `p` = percentage of peers the candidate beats, computed after the direction
  transform of section 10.3.
- `p = 100` means the candidate is at least as strong as every peer on that
  metric.

Percentile is robust, order-preserving and distribution-free. It is the
statistic that drives the family signal (section 12).

### 11.2 Percentile saturates — the envelope

`p` cannot exceed 100, so once a candidate beats every peer, percentile stops
telling us *how far* beyond vanilla it is:

| candidate DPS | peer max | p | peer_max_ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 800 | 720 | 100 | 1.11 |
| 1250 | 720 | 100 | 1.74 |
| 5000 | 720 | 100 | 6.94 |

All three read `percentile: 100`; only the envelope distinguishes them.

Two beyond-envelope values are reported per metric:

```
peer_max_ratio  = candidate / peer_max

envelope_excess = (candidate - peer_max)
                  / max(peer_max - peer_median, epsilon)     (candidate > peer_max)
```

- `peer_max_ratio` is the human-readable form ("1.74 = 74% beyond the strongest
  vanilla peer").
- `envelope_excess` is the calibrated form. Once calibration fixes its
  threshold, it drives the `EXTREME` family state (section 13.1).
- A `saturated` flag is reported when `p == 100`, so downstream code never has
  to infer saturation from a floating-point comparison.

### 11.3 Robust supporting statistics

Reported for context and for `explain` output, not for verdicts:

- `median`, `IQR` — peer centre and spread
- `modified_z` — `0.6745 * (x - median) / MAD`, robust to outliers
- `n_peers` — peer count actually used

`modified_z` is retained but is **not** the decision statistic. Percentile plus
envelope is (section 12.3).

### 11.4 Distribution shape

Before any verdict, the peer distribution is checked for:

- **gaps** — a hole larger than `gap_factor * IQR` between consecutive peers
- **multimodality** — separated clusters, detected on the sorted distribution

A shape problem does not change the numbers. It lowers confidence (section 14)
and is reported, so a human knows the comparison rests on an odd reference set.

### 11.5 Degenerate pools

If `n_peers < k_min` (section 9.5) the entity reports `MANUAL` with reason
`INSUFFICIENT_PEERS`. A verdict is never derived from one or two neighbours.

### 11.6 Worked example

Candidate weapon, selected peers = 12 large energy weapons.

| metric | direction | p | peer max | peer median | peer_max_ratio | envelope_excess | state |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| output_dps | HIGHER_STRONGER | 100 | 720 | 430 | 1.74 | 1.83 | EXTREME |
| reach | HIGHER_STRONGER | 100 | 900 | 600 | 1.11 | 0.33 | ABOVE |
| flux_per_shot | LOWER_STRONGER | 97 | — | — | — | — | ABOVE |
| op_cost | LOWER_STRONGER | 62 | — | — | — | — | IN_BAND |

Two `EXTREME` families would make the entity `EXTREME` (section 13.3); here one
family is `EXTREME` and two are `ABOVE`, which is the `SEVERE` row of the
severity ladder.
